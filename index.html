Presentation framework
RapportOpen
40% of the presentation is Rapport Building
When we get on the call, don’t start closing right away! Go slow, build rapport first.
Spend the first 3-5 minutes building rapport only.
The client must buy you first, the company second, and then products. This cannot go in other order; therefore, you must build rapport in the beginning.
The F.O.R.M method is a great way to begin the rapport building process. Using F.O.R.M gives you topics of conversation to discuss as you are beginning to build rapport:
Family: So are you from this area? Does your family live nearby? What family do you have in this area?
These are the people we collect as referrals later
Occupation: How long have you been at your job? What exactly do you do there?
People like to talk about the way they make their living, you must show a genuine interest in them. This also gives you an idea on household income.
Recreation: What do you do for fun?
Discovering their hobbies may give you something in common with that person. It also shows disposable income and the potential need for insurance if those hobbies are dangerous
Me: Any common bond you share with the client can be used to help build trust with the client, don’t talk on and on about yourself but share your commonality. Such as living in the same area, knowing the same people, or similar hobbies etc.
The rapport building must have a purpose so follow the F.O.R.M guideline.

📚 Product Knowledge

Learn the practical version first: what each product is, who it helps, what problem it solves, and when to bring it up. This is not an underwriting manual and does not replace the brochure or policy contract.

🎯

Product Philosophy

Problems first, products second

Products are solutions, not presentations.

The goal is not to memorize every benefit, rider, or brochure. The goal is to identify a problem and recommend an appropriate solution.

Before discussing a product, ask yourself:

  • What are they worried about?
  • What financial risk exists?
  • What happens if that risk becomes reality?
  • Which product or combination of products helps solve that problem?

The best agents do not lead with products. They lead with questions, uncover needs, and then recommend solutions.

Core product cards

Open each product to study
Final Expense Whole LifePermanent life insurance for final expenses
WL

What it is

Permanent life insurance designed to help protect a family from the financial burden of final expenses. As long as premiums are paid, coverage remains in force.

Who it helps

  • Parents, grandparents, retirees, and homeowners
  • Anyone who does not want loved ones burdened by funeral costs

Problems it solves

  • Funeral home, burial/cremation, cemetery, marker/headstone, and hardship travel expenses
  • Provides cash to beneficiaries to help with end-of-life costs and family needs

New agent note

Do not lead with cash value. Lead with: “If something happened tomorrow, who would be responsible for those expenses?”

Modified LifeLife insurance for clients with health challenges
ALX

What it is

A modified-benefit life insurance product designed for clients who may not qualify for traditional whole life due to health concerns.

Who it helps

  • Clients with significant health conditions
  • Older applicants
  • Applicants declined elsewhere who still need final expense protection

Important benefit structure

Years 1–3 for natural death: premiums paid plus 10% per year. Accidental death can pay the full face amount. Beginning year 4, natural or accidental death pays the face amount.

Never gloss over the modified period.
10RC / 20RC TermRenewable convertible term life protection
10RC / 20RC

What it is

Renewable and convertible term life insurance designed for larger temporary protection needs.

Who it helps

  • Young families
  • Income earners
  • Mortgage holders
  • Parents with dependent children

New agent note

Term generally solves an income problem. Whole Life generally solves a final expense problem.

Mortgage Decreasing TermTerm protection designed around mortgage payoff
15MDT / 30MDT

What it is

A decreasing term policy designed around mortgage protection. The death benefit decreases annually while the premium remains level.

Who it helps

  • Homeowners
  • New home buyers
  • Families carrying significant mortgage debt

New agent note

Do not sell mechanics. Ask: “If something happened, what would you want to happen to the house?”

A supplemental accident policy that pays benefits when covered accidents occur.

  • Families with children
  • Active adults
  • Blue-collar workers
  • Anyone without a large emergency fund

Key benefits

  • Ambulance, emergency treatment, fracture/dislocation, hospital, ICU, transportation, and accidental death benefits
This is accident coverage. Do not present it as sickness coverage.
